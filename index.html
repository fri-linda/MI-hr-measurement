Camera Pulse

Heart rate from a short video of your fingertip (photoplethysmography, PPG). The video is evaluated in this browser and is not uploaded anywhere.

1. Record a video

  1. Open the phone's Camera app, switch to video and turn the light (flash) on.
  2. Cover the rear camera and the flash with your fingertip. Press lightly; pressing hard pushes the blood out and the pulse disappears.
  3. Rest your hand and the phone on a table, keep still and record 20 to 30 seconds.

2. Analyze it

The evaluation takes about as long as the video.

    How the evaluation works
    

      
1. Brightness of every frame

      
For every frame of the video the page takes the central half of the image and averages its red,
        green and blue values. That gives three numbers per frame (about 30 per second), each with the time
        of the frame in the video. With every heartbeat more blood flows into the fingertip, the finger
        absorbs more light and the image gets slightly darker, usually by less than 1 %.

      

        

        
      


      
2. Is the finger on the camera?

      
A frame counts as "finger on the camera" when red is above 60 and at least 1.6 times higher
        than green and blue. The evaluation starts after the finger has covered the camera for 1 second
        and uses at most the following 30 seconds.


      
3. Removing slow changes

      
Pressure of the finger, its warmth or the camera's automatic exposure change the brightness slowly.
        From every value the average of the previous 2 seconds is subtracted, so only fast changes remain.
        The signal is then turned upside down (darker = more blood = up) and smoothed over 0.12 seconds.
        The result is the pulse wave shown in the result.


      
4. Finding the beats

      
A beat is a peak that is the highest point within 0.15 to 0.25 seconds on both sides and rises
        from the lowest point of the previous 0.45 seconds by at least 55 % of the typical rise of the
        last beats. Its time is refined between two frames by a parabola through three points. A second
        peak sooner than 0.3 seconds, or much sooner than usual, is the second hump of the same pulse
        wave (dicrotic notch); only the higher of the two is kept.


      
5. Accepted or rejected

      
The interval between two beats (RR) is accepted (green dot) when it differs by less than
        30 % from the median of the last 8 accepted intervals. Otherwise the beat is rejected
        (orange dot), typically because of movement. Intervals longer than 1.7 seconds (below 35 beats
        per minute) are never accepted.


      
6. Which colour

      
All three colour channels are evaluated this way and the one with the most accepted beats
        (weighted by the share of accepted beats) is used. With the flash on, red is often over-exposed
        (stuck at 255) and the pulse is then clearer in green or blue. A channel that is over-exposed
        for more than half of the time is skipped.


      
7. The numbers in the result

      
Mean BPM = 60 000 / average accepted RR in milliseconds. Min BPM comes from
        the longest accepted RR. SDNN is the standard deviation of the accepted RR intervals and
        RMSSD the root mean square of the differences between consecutive intervals; both describe
        heart rate variability. When the finger was on the camera less than 90 % of the time or fewer
        than 70 % of the beats were accepted, the result is marked as uncertain.
